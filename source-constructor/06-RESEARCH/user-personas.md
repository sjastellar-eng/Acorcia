**Document Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** 2026-03-02

**Owner:** Research Lead

---

## Overview

This document describes the four primary user personas for Source Constructor. These personas were developed through qualitative interviews (n=24), a survey (n=180), and behavioral analysis of comparable platforms. Each persona represents a distinct segment of users who experience the core problem SOC solves: not knowing what to truly work on.

---

## Persona 1: "The Stuck Professional"

### Alex, 31 — Product Manager at a Mid-Size Tech Company

> *"I'm good at my job, but I haven't worked on anything that's truly mine in years. I have a dozen ideas but can't commit to any."*

#### Demographics
| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 28–35 |
| Gender | Any (skews 55% male in research sample) |
| Location | Urban / remote-capable |
| Income | $70k–$120k |
| Education | Bachelor's or higher |
| Relationship | In relationship / married |

#### Psychographics
- High achiever, type A, structured thinker
- Values impact and autonomy over stability
- Reads productivity content (newsletters, podcasts)
- Has tried journaling, therapy, coaching — with mixed results
- Feels the pressure of "I should be building something"

#### Goals
1. Identify the one project that actually excites him
2. Turn vague ambitions into an actionable plan
3. Make consistent daily progress alongside full-time job

#### Frustrations
- Too many options → analysis paralysis
- Can't distinguish between "real desire" and "I should want this"
- Previous tools (Notion templates, productivity apps) don't address the *why*

#### Jobs To Be Done
- "Help me figure out what I actually want to build"
- "Give me a clear roadmap I can execute on evenings and weekends"

#### Discovery Channel
LinkedIn, indie hacker communities (Twitter/X, Reddit r/SideProject), Product Hunt

#### Willingness to Pay
$15–$25/month for a tool that delivers clarity

## Persona 2: "The Career Transitioner"

### Maria, 34 — Former Marketing Manager, Exploring New Direction

> *"I left my job six months ago to 'find my path.' I'm more confused now than when I started. I need someone — or something — to help me think clearly."*

#### Demographics
| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 30–42 |
| Gender | Any (skews 65% female in research sample) |
| Location | Urban, suburban |
| Income | $0–$60k (transitional) |
| Education | Bachelor's or higher |
| Relationship | Various |

#### Psychographics
- Introspective, growth-oriented, self-aware
- Has invested in therapy, coaching, self-help books
- Uses journaling regularly but feels it's circular
- Deeply wants to "do meaningful work" but lacks clarity on what that means
- Highly receptive to AI tools that feel human and thoughtful

#### Goals
1. Clarity on what kind of work lights her up
2. A concrete project to anchor her transition
3. Confidence that she's moving in the right direction

#### Frustrations
- Generic career assessment tools (16personalities, StrengthsFinder) don't translate to action
- Coaching is expensive ($150–300/session) and hard to schedule
- Self-reflection without structure goes in circles

#### Jobs To Be Done
- "Help me discover what I truly want, not what sounds good"
- "Turn my values and strengths into a concrete project"

#### Discovery Channel
Instagram, YouTube, podcasts (Dare to Lead, WorkLife with Adam Grant), coaching communities

#### Willingness to Pay
$20–$35/month — competes with coaching budget

## Persona 3: "The Aspiring Founder"

### Dmitri, 27 — Software Engineer, Wants to Start Something

> *"I can build anything technically. My problem is I keep changing ideas every three weeks. I need to stop chasing shiny objects and commit to one thing."*

#### Demographics
| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 24–33 |
| Gender | Any (skews 70% male in research sample) |
| Location | Global (tech hubs or remote) |
| Income | $80k–$160k |
| Education | CS degree or bootcamp |
| Relationship | Various |

#### Psychographics
- Technically strong, confident in execution
- Weakness: ideation and validation, not building
- Consumes startup content voraciously (YC, Indie Hackers, Lenny's Newsletter)
- Has launched and abandoned 3–7 side projects in past 2 years
- Feels shame about "serial abandoner" pattern

#### Goals
1. Find the idea that's actually aligned with his genuine interests
2. Stop pivoting every month and commit
3. Get early validation quickly, then build

#### Frustrations
- "Build in public" content creates social pressure but not clarity
- Idea validation frameworks feel mechanical without knowing what *he* wants
- AI idea generators produce generic output, not personalized insights

#### Jobs To Be Done
- "Help me identify which of my ideas is worth pursuing based on who I am"
- "Give me a structured first 90 days for my chosen project"

#### Discovery Channel
Hacker News, Twitter/X (tech communities), IndieHackers, ProductHunt

#### Willingness to Pay
$15–$25/month, or annual $120–$200

## Persona 4: "The Curious Life Designer"

### Sofia, 38 — Freelance Designer, Personal Growth Enthusiast

> *"I'm actually pretty happy, but I know there's a bigger project I'm meant to work on. I want to explore that in a structured way."*

#### Demographics
| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 34–45 |
| Gender | Any (skews 60% female in research sample) |
| Location | Urban, lifestyle-flexible |
| Income | $60k–$100k (freelance/contract) |
| Education | Design, humanities, or self-taught |
| Relationship | Various |

#### Psychographics
- Growth-oriented but not desperately unhappy
- Has explored Human Design, Enneagram, IFS, coaching
- Values depth over speed — quality conversations over quick answers
- Very comfortable with AI as a thinking partner
- Community-oriented, likely to share and refer

#### Goals
1. Explore and articulate her "big project" — the thing she's been circling for years
2. Create a structured plan without losing the exploratory feeling
3. Track progress in a way that feels meaningful, not mechanical

#### Frustrations
- Most productivity tools feel soulless and task-focused
- Journaling apps don't help her synthesize insights
- Community accountability apps feel performative

#### Jobs To Be Done
- "Help me explore who I am and what I'm building — without rushing the answer"
- "Capture my insights so I can see patterns over time"

#### Discovery Channel
Substack, Instagram, Podcast communities, word of mouth from coaches/therapists

#### Willingness to Pay
$25–$40/month if the product feels premium and thoughtful

## Primary vs. Secondary Personas

### Primary (Design For)
1. **Alex** — The Stuck Professional
2. **Dmitri** — The Aspiring Founder

### Secondary (Design With)
3. **Maria** — The Career Transitioner
4. **Sofia** — The Curious Life Designer

Design decisions should be validated primarily against Alex and Dmitri, with secondary consideration for Maria and Sofia.

---

## Anti-Personas (Do Not Target)

- **The Pure Entrepreneur:** Someone building a company who already has product-market fit and just wants a project management tool
- **The Student:** Under 22, exploring career — not yet in the life phase SOC addresses
- **The Corporate Manager:** Looking for team productivity tools, not personal clarity
- **The Crisis User:** Someone in acute mental health crisis — SOC is not a mental health tool
