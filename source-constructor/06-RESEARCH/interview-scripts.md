## Overview

This document contains structured interview scripts for user research at different stages:
1. **Discovery Interview** — Understanding the problem space before building
2. **Usability Interview** — Testing the product with real users
3. **Churn Interview** — Understanding why users leave

All interviews are semi-structured. The questions below are a guide, not a rigid script. Follow the participant's lead and go deeper on interesting threads.

---

## Interview 1: Discovery Interview

**Purpose:** Understand the experience of not knowing what to build/pursue and how people currently cope.

**Duration:** 45–60 minutes
**Format:** Video call (Zoom/Meet), recorded with consent
**Participant criteria:** 25–40 years old, employed or self-employed, has had a "side project" idea in the past 12 months

### Pre-Interview Checklist
- [ ] Consent form sent and signed
- [ ] Recording started
- [ ] Screen share off
- [ ] Water/quiet environment confirmed

---

### Script

#### Opening (3 min)

*"Thanks for taking the time to chat with me today. I'm [name] and I'm doing research to understand how people like yourself navigate the challenge of knowing what to work on outside of their main job.*

*There are no right or wrong answers — I'm genuinely curious about your experience. If a question doesn't make sense or doesn't apply to you, just say so and we'll move on.*

*I'd like to record this call for note-taking purposes only. Is that okay with you?"*

---

#### Warm-Up: Context Setting (5 min)

1. Tell me a bit about what you do for work right now.
2. How long have you been in that role?
3. Outside of work, what do you spend most of your time on?

---

#### The Core Problem (15 min)

4. Have you ever had a side project idea — something you wanted to build or explore outside of your main job?
   - *(If yes)* Tell me about the last time you had one of those ideas. What happened?
   - *(If no)* Has there ever been a period where you felt like you *should* be working on something personal but weren't sure what?

5. Walk me through what happened from that initial idea to today. *(Let them narrate)*

6. At what point did you feel most stuck or confused?

7. What did that feeling of being stuck feel like? *(Emotional probe)*

8. Did you ever get the sense that your idea wasn't quite right — that maybe it wasn't what you *truly* wanted?
   - *(Probe)* What made you feel that way?

9. How do you know when you've found an idea worth pursuing vs. one that's just appealing in the moment?

---

#### Current Solutions (10 min)

10. What have you tried to get clarity on what to work on?
    - *(Probe options if needed: journaling, coaching, books, tools, conversations with friends)*

11. Walk me through how that process worked for you. *(For each solution they mention)*

12. What did you like about it? What frustrated you?

13. If you could wave a magic wand and have *any* help getting clarity, what would that look like?

---

#### Values & Motivation (10 min)

14. Think about the times you've felt most alive or energized by what you were working on. What were you doing?

15. What's most important to you about the work you do, beyond money?

16. When you imagine your "ideal project" — one that would excite you to wake up and work on — what qualities would it have?

17. What would working on that project for 6 months look like?

---

#### Closing (5 min)

18. Is there anything about this topic we haven't covered that you think is important?

19. If someone built a tool specifically to help you get clarity on what to pursue — what would have to be true about it for you to pay for it?

20. Do you know anyone else who struggles with this? *(Referral probe)*

*"Thank you so much for your time. This was incredibly helpful. I may follow up with a few questions via email if that's okay."*

## Interview 2: Usability Interview

**Purpose:** Observe users completing core tasks in the product. Identify friction, confusion, and delight moments.

**Duration:** 60 minutes
**Format:** Moderated usability test via screen share
**Participant criteria:** Matches primary persona; has not used SOC before (or fresh prototype session)

---

### Script

#### Introduction (5 min)

*"Today I'm going to ask you to use our product and complete a few tasks. I want to be clear: we're testing the product, not you. There are no wrong moves.*

*As you work through it, I'd like you to think out loud — tell me what you're seeing, what you expect to happen, and what you're feeling. Even if it seems obvious, say it out loud.*

*I won't be able to help you during tasks, but you can ask questions and I'll write them down to answer after."*

---

#### Task 1: First Impression (5 min)

*(Show landing page)*

*"Before you do anything — just look at this page for 30 seconds, then tell me:*
- What do you think this product does?
- Who do you think it's for?
- What would you do first if this were real?"*

---

#### Task 2: Registration & Onboarding (10 min)

*"Please create an account and complete the onboarding."*

Observe:
- [ ] Does user understand each step?
- [ ] Any confusion at the profile questions?
- [ ] Does the "why" behind questions feel clear?
- [ ] Any moments of hesitation or re-reading?

After task: *"What did you think of that? Anything confusing or surprising?"*

---

#### Task 3: Discovery Session (20 min)

*"Now I'd like you to start a Discovery Session — you'll see it in the main menu. Please complete the session as if you were genuinely trying to figure out what to work on."*

Observe:
- [ ] Does user understand what a Discovery Session is?
- [ ] Do the AI questions feel natural or mechanical?
- [ ] Does the user engage deeply or give surface answers?
- [ ] Any moments of "this is exactly what I needed" or frustration?
- [ ] How does the user respond to the session ending?

After task: *"Tell me about that experience. What stood out? What felt off?"*

Probe:
- "Was the AI's tone right for you? Too clinical? Too casual?"
- "Did you feel like the AI understood what you were saying?"
- "Were there any questions that felt odd or intrusive?"

---

#### Task 4: Review Generated Project (10 min)

*"Now I'd like you to look at the project that was generated from your session."*

Observe:
- [ ] First reaction — is it useful, exciting, generic?
- [ ] Does user understand the project structure?
- [ ] Do they want to edit or accept the output?
- [ ] Do they know what to do next?

Questions:
- "Does this feel like *your* project, or something generic?"
- "What would you change, if anything?"
- "If you woke up tomorrow with this project in front of you, would you work on it?"

---

#### Closing (10 min)

Overall reflection:
1. "What's one word you'd use to describe this experience?"
2. "What did you like most?"
3. "What would you change if you could?"
4. "Would you pay for this? If so, how much? If not, why not?"
5. "Who would you show this to?"

## Interview 3: Churn Interview

**Purpose:** Understand why users cancelled or stopped using SOC.

**Duration:** 30 minutes
**Format:** Phone or video call
**Participant criteria:** Cancelled subscription or inactive > 30 days

---

### Script

#### Opening (2 min)

*"Thanks for taking the time. I want to understand your experience honestly — there's no agenda here other than learning. Everything you share helps us improve the product."*

---

#### Usage History (5 min)

1. How did you first hear about Source Constructor?
2. What made you decide to try it?
3. Can you walk me through how you used it in your first week?

---

#### The Turning Point (10 min)

4. When did you start using it less or stop altogether?
5. What was happening in your life at that point?
6. Was there a specific moment or experience that changed things?
7. Did the product fail to deliver on something you expected?

---

#### Comparison (5 min)

8. What do you do now when you need clarity on what to work on?
9. Is there another product or approach you prefer? What does it do better?

---

#### Re-engagement (5 min)

10. What would have to change for you to come back and try it again?
11. Is there a version of this product that *would* keep you engaged?

---

#### Closing (3 min)

12. Is there anything you wish we had asked?

*"Thank you — genuinely. This kind of feedback is the most valuable thing we can receive."*
